Sidereal vs Tropical Astrology: Which Is More Accurate?

By the Natal Notes team. Published November 24, 2026. Sidereal zodiac (Lahiri), times in US Eastern.

The quick read

  • Tropical astrology ties the signs to the seasons; sidereal astrology ties them to the stars behind the Sun.
  • The two zodiacs now sit about 24 degrees apart, so most people's sidereal Sun sign is the sign before their tropical one.
  • Neither is more accurate in general. Each is accurate to the techniques built on it.
  • Vedic tools like nakshatras, dashas and Moon-sign transits only work in the sidereal zodiac, which is why this site uses it.
  • Your Moon and rising signs usually shift too, so check all three before deciding which chart fits you.

Tropical astrology fixes the zodiac to the seasons, starting Aries at the spring equinox. Sidereal astrology fixes it to the stars. Because Earth's axis slowly wobbles, the two now sit about 24 degrees apart, so most people's sidereal signs fall one sign earlier. Neither is "more accurate" across the board; each fits its own techniques.

That's the short answer. The longer one is more interesting, because it explains why your Sun sign might change, why your Moon and rising probably do too, and why Vedic astrology can't simply be done on a Western chart.

What is the difference between sidereal and tropical astrology?

Both zodiacs use the same twelve signs, each exactly 30 degrees wide, laid along the path the Sun appears to travel through the year. The only real difference is where they start counting.

The tropical zodiac starts at the point where the Sun crosses the celestial equator heading north, the March equinox. That moment is 0 degrees Aries by definition, every year. Tropical signs are a map of the seasons: Aries is the start of northern spring, Cancer the summer solstice, Libra the autumn equinox, Capricorn the winter solstice. This is the zodiac of most Western astrology, of horoscope columns, and of the sign you probably grew up knowing.

The sidereal zodiac ("sidereal" means "of the stars") starts from a fixed point against the background stars. In Vedic astrology, that point is set so that the bright star Spica, called Chitra in Sanskrit, sits opposite the start of Aries. Sidereal signs are a map of the sky the Sun is actually standing in front of.

A couple of thousand years ago the two starting points were very close, and it barely mattered which you used. Then they drifted.

Why are the two zodiacs about 24 degrees apart?

Earth spins like a slightly tilted top, and its axis slowly traces a circle. That wobble, called the precession of the equinoxes, makes the equinox point slide backward against the stars by about one degree every 72 years. A full circle takes roughly 26,000 years.

The tropical zodiac moves with the equinox, so it slides too. The sidereal zodiac stays with the stars. The gap between them is called the ayanamsa, and it grows a little every year. By the Lahiri ayanamsa, the standard in Indian astrology and the one this site uses, it is just over 24 degrees right now and widens by less than a minute of arc a year.

There isn't one single sidereal zodiac. Astrologers disagree slightly about the exact starting star, which gives several ayanamsas: Lahiri, Raman, Krishnamurti (used in KP astrology), and Fagan-Bradley (used by Western sidereal astrologers). They differ by roughly a degree, which only matters for a planet sitting right at the edge of a sign.

One thing neither system does is follow the actual constellations, which are uneven in size. Both use twelve equal signs. So the "13th sign" Ophiuchus belongs to neither zodiac.

Which is more accurate, sidereal or tropical?

This is the question people really want answered, so here it is plainly: there is no neutral test that crowns one zodiac. Astrology isn't something controlled research has validated, under either system. What you can ask is which zodiac is accurate to the thing it measures, and which one fits the techniques you're using.

  • If "accurate" means matching the sky, sidereal wins. When a sidereal chart says the Sun is in Sagittarius, the Sun really is in front of the stars of that part of the sky.
  • If "accurate" means matching the seasons, tropical wins. Tropical Aries is always the start of spring in the north, which is the symbolism Western astrology inherited and built on.
  • If "accurate" means "my readings describe me," you have to test it yourself. Plenty of people feel their sidereal Sun sign never fit, and plenty feel the opposite.

The deeper point is that each system's tools were built for its own zodiac. Nakshatras, the 27 lunar mansions of 13°20' each, begin at the first degree of sidereal Aries. Vimshottari dashas, the planetary periods that structure a Vedic reading, are calculated from your Moon's sidereal nakshatra. Gochara, reading transits from your natal Moon sign, assumes sidereal positions. Run those on a tropical chart and you get the wrong nakshatra, the wrong dasha and the wrong houses. Western techniques, meanwhile, were developed and tested by their practitioners in tropical terms.

So the fairest verdict is this: pick the system whose methods you want to use, and use its zodiac all the way through. We work sidereally because the depth of Vedic astrology, the nakshatras, the dashas, the Moon-sign forecasts, lives there.

What is my sidereal sign?

Your sidereal Sun sign depends on when the Sun crosses into each sidereal sign. Those dates shift by a day or so from year to year, and they run roughly three weeks later than the tropical dates you know. Here is where the Sun changes sign in the sidereal zodiac over the coming year, in US Eastern time:

  • Capricorn: the Sun enters sidereal Capricorn on January 14, 2027.

  • Pisces: the Sun enters sidereal Pisces on March 14, 2027.
  • Aries: the Sun enters sidereal Aries on April 14, 2027.
  • Taurus: the Sun enters sidereal Taurus on May 15, 2027.

  • Libra: the Sun enters sidereal Libra on October 17, 2027.

The rule of thumb: if you were born in roughly the first three weeks of your tropical sign, your sidereal Sun is the sign before it. A tropical Leo born early in Leo season is usually a sidereal Cancer. Born in the last week or so of your tropical sign, you usually keep it. Near a changeover, your birth year and time decide it, so use a calculator rather than a list.

Do my Moon sign and rising sign change too?

Usually, and they matter more than the Sun in Vedic astrology. Every planet and point in your chart moves back by the same 24 degrees or so, so the same rule applies to each one: if it sat in roughly the first 24 degrees of a tropical sign, it moves into the previous sign sidereally.

Your Moon sign (rashi) is the anchor of a Vedic chart. Monthly transits, Sade Sati and your dashas are all read from it, which is why our horoscopes are written for your Moon sign first. Your rising sign sets the houses, so if it shifts, every house shifts with it. The order of the houses doesn't change; only which sign sits on each one.

That's why some people find their sidereal chart suddenly makes sense: a tropical Gemini Sun who never felt like a Gemini may turn out to be a sidereal Taurus with a sidereal Cancer Moon, and that's the chart that rings true.

Try both tonight

Find your sidereal Sun, Moon and rising, then write down your tropical ones next to them. Under each pair, answer one question: which description sounds more like how you actually move through the world, not who you wish you were? Do the Moon first. Most people find that one decides it.

How should I read a sidereal horoscope?

Read it for your sidereal Moon sign first, then again for your sidereal rising sign. Skip your tropical Sun sign entirely; it isn't the sign Vedic forecasts are written for. Each of our monthly forecasts counts the houses from the Moon, the traditional way, and gives the exact dates, degrees and nakshatras behind every theme. If you write in a sign journal, use the one for your sidereal Moon: it's the sign that describes your inner weather.

Questions

Why is my sidereal sign different from my tropical sign?

Because the two zodiacs start in different places. Tropical Aries begins at the spring equinox point, while sidereal Aries begins where the constellations sit, and the slow wobble of Earth's axis has moved those two starting points about 24 degrees apart.

Is sidereal astrology more accurate than tropical?

Neither zodiac is more accurate on its own terms. The sidereal zodiac lines up with the stars, the tropical zodiac lines up with the seasons, and each tradition built its techniques on its own zodiac. The honest test is which system's readings describe your life better.

Can I use both sidereal and tropical astrology?

Yes, as long as you keep each one's methods together. Read Vedic techniques like nakshatras and dashas from a sidereal chart, and Western techniques from a tropical chart. Mixing a tropical chart with Vedic rules is where most confusion comes from.

Does my rising sign change in sidereal astrology?

Often, yes. Every point in the chart moves back about 24 degrees, so if your tropical rising sign is in its first two thirds or so, your sidereal rising sign is the one before it. Your houses keep the same order around the chart.

Which sidereal ayanamsa does Natal Notes use?

Lahiri, also called Chitrapaksha, the standard in Indian astrology. Other ayanamsas such as Raman or Krishnamurti differ from it by roughly a degree, which only changes your sign if a planet sits right at the edge of one.
